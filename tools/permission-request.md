# A note you could send World Coffee Research

The Sensory Lexicon carries no licence — only *"Copyright © 2017 World Coffee
Research. All rights reserved."* So Roast Coach ships the attribute names,
categories and reference intensities, which are facts about a measuring
instrument, and asks each roaster to import the definitions from their own
downloaded copy.

If WCR say yes to the note below, the definitions can ship with the app and the
import step disappears. Their variety catalogue is already CC BY-NC-ND, so they
are plainly not hostile to this kind of use — the Lexicon may simply never have
had a licence chosen for it.

Send to the contact address on worldcoffeeresearch.org.

---

**Subject:** Permission to include Sensory Lexicon definitions in a free
open-source roasting app

Hello,

I roast on an Aillio Bullet and have built a small open-source app that analyses
my roast profiles and helps me record what I taste. It is free, not sold, and the
source is public.

I would like it to carry the Sensory Lexicon's attribute definitions so that when
I record a tasting note the app can show what the attribute means, in your words —
which is the whole value of a lexicon, and the reason two roasteries' notes can be
compared at all.

At the moment the app includes only the attribute names, their categories and
their reference intensity values, with the Lexicon cited as the source and a link
to your download page. Each user imports the definitions from their own copy of
your PDF, because the Lexicon states all rights reserved and I did not want to
assume.

May I include the definitions directly, with attribution? I am happy to:

- carry your copyright notice and a link to worldcoffeeresearch.org wherever the
  lexicon appears;
- mark the definitions clearly as yours rather than the app's;
- keep the app free and noncommercial, and come back to you first if that ever
  changes;
- remove them at once if you would rather I did not.

If a licence like the CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 you use for the Varieties Catalog would
suit, that would answer it completely and I would follow those terms.

Thank you for publishing the Lexicon — it is the reason a roaster's notes can
mean the same thing to somebody else.

Best regards,
<your name>
<link to the repository>
