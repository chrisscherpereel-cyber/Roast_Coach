Roast Coach

Setting up the database

Half an hour, once, and then roasts arrive on their own — on every computer, without anybody uploading anything. Nothing here costs money.

Your Mac
RoasTime saves each roast. A small helper notices and sends it on.
→
The database
A free filing cabinet on the internet. It just holds the roasts.
→
Roast Coach
Reads the cabinet and shows you what to change next.

The three pieces never talk to each other any other way. That is what makes it work from any computer, and what makes the browser's rules about folders stop mattering.

STEP ONEMake the free database

  1. Go to supabase.com and sign up. A Google or GitHub account works.
  2. Press New project.
  3. Give it any name — Roast Coach is fine.
  4. It asks you to make up a database password. Make one up, and save it somewhere — a password manager, a note. You will need it in a minute and Supabase will not show it to you again.
  5. Choose the region closest to you.
  6. Press create, and wait a minute or two while it builds.
!
Use letters and numbers in that password. If it contains @ : / or #, it breaks the address you paste later.

You never have to open Supabase again after the next step. Roast Coach builds its own tables the first time it connects — there is nothing to set up inside the database itself.

STEP TWOCopy the address

This is the one line that tells everything else where the cabinet is.

  1. In your new project, press Connect at the top of the page.
  2. A panel opens with several options. Find Session pooler.
  3. Copy the line underneath it. It looks like this:
postgresql://postgres.abcdefghijk:[YOUR-PASSWORD]@aws-1-us-east-2.pooler.supabase.com:5432/postgres
  1. Paste it into a note, and replace [YOUR-PASSWORD] — square brackets and all — with the password you made up in step one.
!
Take Session pooler, not Direct connection. They look almost identical and only one of them works. The direct one uses a kind of internet address that Streamlit's servers cannot reach, and the error it gives you — "cannot assign requested address" — tells you nothing about why. If you ever see that, you took the wrong one.

STEP THREETell the app

Roast Coach keeps this address in a private settings box that is never part of the public code, so your app and your repository can both stay public while your roasts stay yours.

  1. Go to share.streamlit.io and find your app.
  2. Press the ⋮ beside it → Settings → Secrets.
  3. Type this into the box, with your own address on the second line:
[database]
url = "postgresql://postgres.abcdefghijk:mypassword@aws-1-us-east-2.pooler.supabase.com:5432/postgres"
  1. Keep the quotation marks. Press Save. The app restarts by itself.

Open the app and go to the Data page. Near the bottom it says where it is keeping things. It should now say Postgres, not SQLite. If it still says SQLite, the section heading is misspelled — it has to be exactly [database].

STEP FOURMake sign-ins

Your app has a public web address, so it needs a lock on the door. Open password_tool.html from the download — just double-click it — and add a name and password for each person. It hands you a block of text to paste into the same Secrets box, underneath the database part.

The passwords are scrambled in your own browser before they are written down, and nothing is sent anywhere. What ends up in Secrets cannot be turned back into a password.

Adding somebody later means pasting the whole block again with their line included — so keep a copy in your password manager.

STEP FIVETurn on automatic syncing

This is the part that means you never upload anything again. It runs on the Mac that has RoasTime on it, and takes about a minute.

Do not double-click it — macOS blocks files that came from a download, and the old right-click trick no longer works. Use Terminal instead, which has no such problem:

  1. Open Terminal — ⌘-Space, type Terminal, press Return.
  2. Type bash followed by a space.
  3. Open the download's mac folder in Finder and drag setup.command into the Terminal window. It fills in the path for you.
  4. Press Return.

It asks you one question — paste the same address from step two — and then does the rest: checks it can see your roasts, installs what it needs, sends everything across once so you can see it worked, and offers to keep doing that every fifteen minutes and whenever you log in.

Say yes, and that is the end of it. Roast on the Bullet, and the roast turns up in Roast Coach by itself, for everybody.

!
Why not double-click? Apple removed the right-click → Open bypass in macOS Sequoia, so a downloaded script simply refuses to run and there is no obvious way through. Terminal is not affected: Gatekeeper stops the Finder from launching the file, but you are handing it to the shell yourself.

If you would rather double-click anyway: try it, let it be blocked, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, and press Open Anyway beside the message about setup.command. The message only appears for a short while after the attempt.

Only one Mac needs to do this — whichever one you roast from. It keeps everybody's view current.

How to tell it worked

WhereWhat you should see
The app's Data page “Postgres — aws-1-…supabase.com”, in green, under Where this is stored
The app's roast count Goes up on its own within fifteen minutes of a roast, with nobody uploading
Another computer Signs in and sees exactly the same roasts
~/Library/Logs/roast-coach-sync.log A line for each check: “1 new → Postgres”, or “nothing new”

If something goes wrong

What it saysWhat it means
cannot assign requested address You took the Direct connection. Go back and take the Session pooler one.
password authentication failed Either [YOUR-PASSWORD] is still sitting in the line, or the password has a character like @ in it that breaks the address.
The Data page still says SQLite The Secrets box is missing the [database] heading, or it is spelled differently.
No accounts are set up yet The [passwords] part has not been pasted in yet. Step four.
macOS will not open setup.command Expected for a downloaded file, and right-click → Open no longer helps. Open Terminal, type bash and a space, drag the file in, press Return.
The roasts went across but the schedule would not start Your roasts are in the database — only the repeating failed. The script now says what macOS objected to, and falls back to the older scheduler by itself.
Everything is slow first thing Normal. The free database goes to sleep when unused and takes a few seconds to wake up.

What it costs: nothing. Supabase gives away 500 MB, which at roughly 20 KB a roast is tens of thousands of them, and Streamlit is free for public apps. Neither asks for a card. The free database pauses after a week of nobody opening it, and wakes up on its own the next time you do.

Full technical details, including moving roasts you already have, are in SETUP.md in the download.
