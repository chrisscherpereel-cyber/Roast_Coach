# Reading the WCR Coffee Varieties Catalog as a roaster

## How varieties are grouped genetically

WCR sorts *Coffea arabica* into five families:

- **Ethiopian landrace** — locally adapted populations from arabica's centre of
  origin (Geisha, Java, AB3). Very high cup quality, low yields.
- **Bourbon–Typica group**, split into *Typica related*, *Bourbon related*, and
  *Typica and Bourbon related*. A handful of trees taken out of Yemen from the late
  1600s underpins most world production — a real genetic bottleneck. In Brazil,
  97.55% of cultivars descend from these two.
- **Introgressed (Catimor / Sarchimor / Other)** — arabica carrying *C. canephora*
  genes, nearly always via the Timor Hybrid. Catimor = Timor Hybrid × Caturra;
  Sarchimor = Timor Hybrid × Villa Sarchi. Neither is a variety; each is a family.
- **F1 hybrid (introgressed / not introgressed)** — first-generation crosses of two
  distinct arabica parents. High yield and quality, but seed segregates, so they must
  be propagated clonally.

Robusta is grouped by geographic origin instead: **Guinea**, **Congo**, **Uganda**,
**Guinea × Congo**, **Guinea × *C. congensis***.

## What "quality potential" means — and its caveats

The field is **quality potential at high altitude**, rated *Very Low, Low, Good, Very
Good, Exceptional*. It is a **ceiling under ideal conditions**, not a cup score.

1. It is **relative**. WCR anchors the scale to reference varieties — Caturra in
   Central America, SL28 in Africa — and expects you to calibrate against those.
2. It is **conditional on altitude**. Grown low, a variety will not reach its rating.
3. Environment, soil nutrition, tree age and management "can significantly affect a
   coffee tree's yield, quality, and health." Only six varieties rate *Exceptional*.

## Traits that matter to a roaster

There is **no density field**, so read density indirectly from **optimal altitude**,
given as a band (Low/Medium/High) rather than metres because the metre equivalent
depends on latitude (High = >1600 m at the equator, >1000 m above 15°). High-band
varieties give harder, denser beans that need more energy in.

**Bean size** is the other lever: *Below Average / Average / Large / Very Large*,
with Caturra = Average, SL28 = Large, Maragogipe = Very Large. Very Large beans
(Maragogipe, Pacamara, Anacafe 14) roast unevenly against a normal screen
distribution. Robusta bean size is stated as screen size instead.

Also read **ripening of fruit** (lot uniformity) and the **rust caveat**: because
most resistance traces to one shared Timor Hybrid parent, WCR expects "most existing
rust-resistant varieties will no longer be resistant in the near-to-medium term"
(Lempira and Costa Rica 95 already broke down). T5175, T5296, Anacafe 14 and Pacamara
are neither uniform nor stable, so lots under those names vary.
